Research

The wordsalreadysaid it.

We hid every aspect in Twitter-2015 and Twitter-2017 and trained four models over three seeds. With the subject gone, the text model barely noticed, and adding the photo gave no significant gain.

Every model that reads the text lands in the same place

Test macro-F1 (%), mean of three seeds. Whiskers show one standard deviation.

Why the photo added little

Three things inthe data

NegPos

One photo, several aspects

Many test images belong to two or more aspects with different labels. A model that only sees the image must give them all one answer, which caps it at 89.6% and 76.7% accuracy.

PERORGLOC

Aspects are named entities

A photo of a person or a stadium rarely shows which named entity the author means, so the picture can't easily fill in the hidden name.

Dinner at <mask>, absolutely delicious!

The opinion words stay

Masking removes the name but not the words around it, and the text encoder was already trained on tweet sentiment. Most of the mood is still in the sentence.

Read with care

Limitations

  • Implicit aspects are simulated by masking; no naturally implicit posts were annotated.
  • The aspect-type task predicts an entity type, not the hidden words, and 8 to 10% of Twitter-2015 samples could not be labelled.
  • Three seeds can't resolve very small differences, and the ViT is frozen and trained on generic photos.
  • Results come from English tweets about named entities and may not carry over to product reviews, where the photo often shows the aspect itself.
