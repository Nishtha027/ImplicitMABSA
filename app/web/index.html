Read the word a tweet doesn't say.

Give it a tweet and its photo. ImplicitMABSA hides the subject, then infers how the author feels about it and what kind of thing it is, from the words left behind and 196 patches of the image.

Try the model See how it works

Four steps from tweet to prediction

  1. Dinner at<mask>tonight

    Hide the aspect

    If you name the aspect and it appears in the tweet, it is replaced by a mask token. The model never sees it.

  2. Read the words

    A Twitter RoBERTa encoder, fine-tuned on this task, turns the remaining words into token states.

  3. Read the photo

    A frozen Vision Transformer cuts the image into 14 × 14 = 196 patches and encodes each one.

  4. Fuse and predict

    Text and image features are combined, and two heads predict the sentiment and the aspect's type.

What the research found

We hid every aspect in the Twitter-2015 and Twitter-2017 benchmarks and compared image-only, text-only, concatenation and gated cross-attention models over three seeds. The words left in a tweet turned out to carry almost everything: hiding the aspect cost the text model under two points, and the photo did not add a significant gain on these benchmarks.

  • 72.8%macro-F1 on Twitter-2015 with the aspect hidden
  • 0.4 ptslost by hiding the aspect on Twitter-2015
  • 66 runstrained in 107 minutes on one T4 GPU

ImplicitMABSA, a research project at Bennett University by Nishtha Jain, Aman Swamy and Tamanna Arora.
